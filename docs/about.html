About this tracker

What this is

A quarterly, freely available record of funding, company formation and collaboration across the global quantum technology sector, published by ECIPE and computed directly from the underlying data rather than maintained by hand.

Data & methodology

The tracker records companies whose core activity is quantum technology, classifies each on two independent axes, and records their disclosed funding history round by round. The aim is coverage as wide as possible, while also ensuring that non-quantum companies are not included. It is a work in progress and is improved continuously.

What counts as a quantum company. A company whose core products, services, components or R&D materially develop, commercialise, integrate or enable technologies that exploit quantum physics to achieve functions or performance not attainable with classical technologies. Four areas qualify: core quantum technologies; quantum software and methods, including quantum-derived computation that runs on classical hardware; components, equipment and materials designed for quantum systems; and post-quantum cryptography framed against the threat from quantum computing. The test is the quantum function, not the underlying physics – every transistor and laser obeys quantum mechanics, so a device whose only quantum content is its operating principle does not qualify, however it is branded. Generic activity in photonics, AI, cybersecurity, semiconductors, optics, electronics, cryogenics or materials is not included unless it enables quantum systems specifically.

How companies are classified. Two independent axes. The pillar records the technology domain: Quantum Computing and Simulation; Quantum Communication and Networking; Quantum Sensing and Metrology; Enabling Technologies; Quantum-Safe Security / PQC. The stack layer records position in the value chain: Materials & Fabrication; Components & Control; Core Quantum Hardware; Software & Platforms; Applications & Services; and Full Stack, reserved for companies that build the qubit platform, the control and readout layer that operates it and the software users program it through. Within its layer, each company also carries a stream – a finer category naming what it actually makes.

How funding is recorded. A round is recorded where a source describes it as capital raised by the company or as public funding. Procurement and commercial contracts are not funding. Public support for research and development counts whether it is delivered as a grant or as a contract; money that pays for an already developed product does not. Each round keeps its source label as a round type, which maps to one of seven aggregated funding stages and four financing instruments. All values are in nominal USD.

What to keep in mind. Company funding means capital raised by the quantum companies themselves; the in-house quantum spending of large corporations is not measured. Grant coverage is uneven because grant transparency and quantum activity barely correlate – Germany, Japan, South Korea, Israel, Switzerland and Singapore publish no usable recipient-level data, so public funding to companies based there is likely understated. Chinese sources are thinner, so the figures recorded from them are deliberately conservative: where an equity round is reported only as a magnitude, a floor is recorded – CNY 20 million for “tens of millions” of yuan, CNY 200 million for “hundreds of millions”.

The full methodology is on GitHub.

Company funding is all capital raised by the tracked companies, across every instrument (VC and private equity, debt, grants, public equity). Government funding is a separate measure: money announced or deployed through national and supranational quantum policy programmes, sourced from ECIPE's own policy register. It does not include grants or public equity that reached companies through funding rounds – that money is counted only once, under Company funding.

Panels marked Illustrative · mock data use placeholder figures pending publication of the underlying analysis and should not be cited. Government funding figures are currently marked Provisional for a different reason: the numbers are real, but the treatment of overlapping multi-year programmes is not yet settled, so national totals for the largest funders are upper bounds.
