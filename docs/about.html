About this tracker

What this is

A quarterly, freely available record of funding, company formation and collaboration across the global quantum technology sector, published by ECIPE and computed directly from the underlying data rather than maintained by hand.

Before you cite

  • Panels marked Illustrative data use placeholder figures pending publication of the underlying analysis and should not be cited.
  • The figures show identified funding, not totals. A lower figure can reflect less disclosure rather than less funding.
  • Company funding and government funding are separate measures and are not added together.

Companies

The tracker records companies whose core activity is quantum technology, classifies each on two independent axes, and records their disclosed funding history round by round. The aim is coverage as wide as possible, while also ensuring that non-quantum companies are not included. It is a work in progress and is improved continuously.

What counts as a quantum company. A company whose core products, services, components or R&D materially develop, commercialise, integrate or enable technologies that exploit quantum physics to achieve functions or performance not attainable with classical technologies. Four areas qualify:

  • core quantum technologies;
  • quantum software and methods, including quantum-derived computation that runs on classical hardware;
  • components, equipment and materials designed for quantum systems;
  • post-quantum cryptography framed against the threat from quantum computing.

The test is the quantum function, not the underlying physics: every transistor and laser obeys quantum mechanics, so a device whose only quantum content is its operating principle does not qualify, however it is branded. Generic activity in photonics, AI, cybersecurity, semiconductors, optics, electronics, cryogenics or materials is not included unless it enables quantum systems specifically.

How companies are classified. Two independent axes. The pillar records the technology domain:

  • Quantum Computing and Simulation
  • Quantum Communication and Networking
  • Quantum Sensing and Metrology
  • Enabling Technologies
  • Quantum-Safe Security / PQC (post-quantum cryptography)

The stack layer records position in the value chain:

  • Materials & Fabrication
  • Components & Control
  • Core Quantum Hardware
  • Software & Platforms
  • Applications & Services
  • Full Stack, reserved for companies that build the qubit platform, the control and readout layer that operates it and the software users program it through.

Within its layer, each company also carries a stream, a finer category naming what it actually makes.

How company funding is recorded. Company funding is all capital raised by the tracked companies themselves, across every financing instrument (VC / private equity, debt, grant and public equity). A round is recorded where a source describes it as capital raised by the company or as public funding. Procurement and commercial contracts are not funding. Public support for research and development counts whether it is delivered as a grant or as a contract; money that pays for an already developed product does not.

Each round keeps its source label as a round type (for example, Series B). The round type maps to one of seven aggregated funding stages (Series B is Early-stage equity) and to one of four financing instruments (Series B is VC / private equity).

What the company figures do not capture. The in-house quantum spending of large corporations is not measured. Grant coverage is uneven because grant transparency and quantum activity barely correlate: Germany, Japan, South Korea, Israel, Switzerland and Singapore publish no usable recipient-level data, so public funding to companies based there is likely understated. Chinese sources are thinner, so the figures recorded from them are deliberately conservative: where an equity round is reported only as a magnitude, a floor is recorded, namely CNY 20 million for “tens of millions” of yuan and CNY 200 million for “hundreds of millions”.

Public funding for quantum technologies

Government funding is a separate measure from company funding: identified public funding and funding commitments for quantum technologies, covering national and supranational programmes and sourced from ECIPE's public funding dataset. It does not include grants or public equity that reached companies through funding rounds; that money is counted only once, under company funding.

Public funding is analysed across 36 jurisdictions, with the EU treated separately as a supranational source of funding. The data reflects information verified up to 29 September 2026.

Why comparison is difficult. Comparing government support for quantum technologies is not straightforward. There is no common international accounting framework, and governments fund quantum activities through very different channels: national strategies, research programmes, infrastructure projects, procurement, regional initiatives and public investment institutions. Reporting practices also vary widely. Some governments publish detailed programme budgets, while others disclose only broad multi-year commitments or provide little technology-specific information.

A bottom-up approach. Rather than relying primarily on headline estimates, the dataset records funding where a specific monetary amount can be linked to an identifiable public funding measure for quantum technologies and where its relationship with other funding measures can be assessed with sufficient confidence. The aim is to measure what can be observed, attributed and verified.

What is included. Public quantum funding includes support attributable to governments, public authorities, public research bodies, public financial institutions and supranational programmes. Funding is included where the activity is specifically directed towards quantum technologies or where a quantum component can be identified separately within a broader programme. General technology programmes covering quantum alongside areas such as artificial intelligence or semiconductors are excluded when the quantum share cannot be isolated. Private corporate and philanthropic funding are not included.

Core and Additional funding. Core funding captures the principal national or federal quantum effort, including major programmes, research funding streams and national infrastructure investments. Additional funding captures identifiable support outside this main funding architecture, including regional initiatives, stand-alone projects, later programme additions, supplementary infrastructure and public equity, loans or other investment capital. Funding is also classified by type: national or regional programmes; research and innovation support; infrastructure and procurement; public equity, loans and investment; and other quantum-specific public funding.

Funding status and periods. Governments disclose quantum funding at different stages of the funding process, and the figures therefore include realised expenditure, awards, allocations, appropriations, commitments, announced or planned funding, pending allocations and identifiable budget requests. These categories are not economically or legally equivalent. Inclusion does not mean that an amount has already been appropriated, formally committed or spent, and funding status is taken into account when interpreting the results. Funding periods also vary substantially, from a single year of spending to multi-year strategies, so country totals should not be read as annual spending. Public loans and equity are included where the public contribution can be identified, but remain separately classified because they are not economically equivalent to grants.

Avoiding double counting. Individual grants, projects or infrastructure investments are not added separately when they are already financed from a larger programme included elsewhere in the dataset. Subsequent funding is counted only where there is evidence that it represents genuinely additional resources. For co-financed projects, only the identifiable contribution of the relevant public authority is attributed to that jurisdiction. Where overlap cannot be resolved with confidence, the uncertainty is recorded rather than resolved by assumption.

The EU is reported separately because EU-level programmes represent genuine public funding while also financing activities carried out in individual Member States. The EU figure should therefore not simply be added to national figures when constructing an aggregate measure of European public support.

Currency conversion. All observations are retained in their original currencies and converted into nominal US dollars using the relevant annual exchange rate, with the 2026 year-to-date average used for observations from 2026.

How to read the figures. The figures show identified public funding and funding commitments, not total government spending, and a lower figure can reflect less public disclosure rather than less funding. This applies in particular to China, where the recorded figure of approximately $2B covers only funding that can be traced to specific programmes and does not capture several important post-2019 channels whose value is not publicly disclosed.

Glossary

Aggregated funding stage
How mature a company is when it raises a round: Seed and Angel, Early-stage equity, Late-stage equity, Growth equity, Public equity, Debt or Grant. Overview Figure 5 shows the equity ladder, in which late-stage and growth equity are combined and debt and grants are excluded.
Archetype
One of four groups of countries in Occasional Paper 08/2025: Global Innovation Hubs, Research Networkers, Regional Commercial Leaders and Emerging Ecosystems. A country's archetype is the quadrant it falls in when plotted by global connectedness and industry-involving collaborations to GDP (Countries, Figure 6).
Cluster
A place where quantum companies and institutions are concentrated, and which clears minimum thresholds for funding and institutional density. Clusters are ranked on Market Orientation, Collaboration Intensity and Ecosystem Maturity (Clusters tab).
Collaboration
One pair of institutions working together, counted once however many publications or announcements support it, and attributed to the two institutions' countries.
Connectedness, industry-involving collaborations to GDP
Global connectedness is a country's degree centrality: the share of all other countries it has at least one cross-border collaboration with. Industry-involving collaborations to GDP counts the collaborations in which at least one partner is a company, per trillion USD of GDP, as a proxy for commercial intensity (Countries, Figure 6).
Financing instrument
The kind of money in a funding round. Four are used. VC / private equity: equity and equity-linked financing of private companies, from angel and seed rounds to growth rounds, including convertible notes issued while private. Debt: borrowed money, including debt issued after an IPO. Grant: non-repayable public funding. Public equity: equity raised on public markets, such as IPOs and placements after an IPO.
GDP
Gross domestic product in current US dollars, from the World Bank (World Development Indicators, indicator NY.GDP.MKTP.CD), for 2025. A country the World Bank has not yet published for 2025 uses its latest earlier year. Taiwan, which the World Bank does not cover, uses a figure carried over from an earlier version of this tracker. GDP is the denominator of the funding-to-GDP views and of industry-involving collaborations to GDP (Countries, Figure 6).
Nominal US dollars
Dollar values in the year they were recorded, not adjusted for inflation.
Pillar, stack layer, stream
Defined above, under How companies are classified.
PQC (post-quantum cryptography)
Encryption designed to resist attack by quantum computers.
Provisional
Applied to government funding figures that are still under review. Entries with soft qualifiers (such as proposed or up to), draft or superseded status, blended public-private amounts or several amounts in one cell are excluded until reviewed.
Quasi-cluster
A place with real quantum activity that has not yet cleared the funding or institutional-density bar for full cluster status.
RCA (revealed comparative advantage)
Defined under Countries, Figure 7.
Round type
The label a source gives a funding round, such as Seed, Series B, Grant or Post-IPO equity. Each round type maps to one funding stage and one financing instrument.
Specialisation index
A country's share of companies in one stack layer divided by that layer's share of all tracked companies. Above 1.0 means proportionally more companies in the layer than the world. Defined under Countries, Figure 7.
Spinout
A company that originated from a university, research organisation or corporation.
